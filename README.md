# dsan_5550_project
Final project for Data Science and Climate Change. Elective course in the Georgetown University MS in Data Science and Analytics program.

## Project Description
Despite growing concerns about climate change, especially after UNEP’s report that the globe will most likely cross 1.5°C of warming, many consumers find it difficult to choose sustainable products. An article by Emma Prouteau for the World Economic Forum highlights a few reasons for this difficulty, including higher prices for sustainable products, convenience, the cognitive load of researching new products, and social norms surrounding product choice. These broad issue categories apply to car shopping as consumers debate the “pros and cons” of switching from typical gas cars (Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) vehicles) to hybrid or electric vehicles (EVs).

Prouteau, E. (2025, March 15). Why consumers still struggle to buy sustainable products. World Economic Forum. https://www.weforum.org/stories/nature-and-biodiversity/consumers-sustainability-choices-world-consumer-rights-day

This project will include an interactive website where users can input preferences like budget, body type, and willingness to construct personal EV infrastructure. The website will calculate a list of best-fitting cars for users to research (based on data retrieved from CarAPI). All cars in the database will also be scored on their sustainability. This will allow consumers to begin their own research with a list of cars that are in their budget, with a view of each car's comparative sustainability. The goal of this project is to demonstrate whether users can make sustainable choices in car buying with their budget.


## Instructor's description:
The final project will be an application of a DS / ML / AI method (of the student’s choice) to some aspect of climate change (also of the student’s choice).  The project will be done individually.  Students will first present a proposal before undertaking the project.  

The project should include all relevant aspects of the DS / ML / AI pipeline (e.g. problem definition, data collection and refinement, implementation, evaluation, and discussion).

Students will summarize their project on a poster.  ​​They will submit a voice-over video of up to 5 minutes describing their poster and optionally demonstrating their project, as if presenting at a conference poster session. 

The project deliverables will include:
an initial and revised project proposal (1-2 pages)
a documented code repository
e.g. github and/or google colab and/or a notebook, as appropriate
a poster
a video (up to 5 minutes) describing the poster and optionally demonstrating the project

In place of a final exam, students will write up their final project as a paper (5-10 pages) suitable for submission to a conference or workshop.  Students must use the NeurIPS style files, which will make it easier to submit the paper to a conference or workshop.  (This will also require learning (if not already known), how to use LaTeX, which is a valuable skill; introductions can be found online.)  The paper should be submitted as a PDF.

Both the poster and paper should document the carbon cost of the project, e.g. any model training and inference.
